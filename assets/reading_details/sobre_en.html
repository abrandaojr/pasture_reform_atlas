How to read the Atlas

StepWhat to doFollow the example
1. Check the place and datesChoose a municipality. Read the year beside each value.Follow a fictional municipality with 100 ha of pasture and 200 cattle. All numbers in this example are illustrative.
2. Understand cattle farming sizeHectares measure area; head counts measure animals. Compare each measure across municipalities.100 ha is the pasture area. 200 head is the herd. These totals alone do not show production efficiency.
3. See how much pasture could improveRead the area and percentage of low- and medium-vigor pasture together.If 60 of the 100 ha have low or medium vigor, that is 60% of the pasture. This points to where recovery should be investigated.
4. Check the conditionsReview slaughterhouse access, drought, technical assistance and education. Open “Explain” for each calculation.If 30 of the 100 ha with calculated distances fall within the regional threshold, access is 30%. The threshold is the median pasture-to-slaughterhouse distance in the mesoregion.
5. Examine environmental pressureRead the five environmental indicators separately, then the summary.If three indicators are in the very high class, three signals need attention. This does not mean 60% of the municipality has a problem.
6. Read the legend and missing dataCheck what colors mean on each map. More bar segments mean greater intensity, not a larger percentage.The 60% pasture share is an observed value. The class compares it with other municipalities. A missing indicator can make a result partial; missing data do not become zero.
7. Connect properties and the supply chainConsult CAR registrations, distances and movement records. Open the sources to check coverage.A slaughterhouse near the 100 ha shows geographic access. Establishing whether it receives cattle from those properties requires records documenting that relationship.
8. Decide what to investigate nextUse the profile to choose where to investigate further. Conclusions about an individual farm require a separate assessment.In this example, we would investigate the 60 ha of low- or medium-vigor pasture, considering local conditions and environmental signals before recommending an intervention.
